03 · Style review

A nine wearing a six.

The identity is right. Black ground, cream type, one metallic, a serif pair with wide tracking, one italic gold word per heading. Packaging and site speak the same language, which most small brands never manage. What breaks the premium read is consistency, not concept.

  1. Two metallics fixThe theme and the packaging are gold. The Founder page, the v4 theme and the Chapter One card are rose copper. Trusted By, FAQs and the gate are gold again. Pick the metal that is on the jar foil and use only that on the site. Copper can live on the card and on limited touches.
  2. Three type systems fixTheme pages set Cormorant + EB Garamond. The Founder page adds IBM Plex Mono, which works. Rebuild those pages on theme sections or copy the Founder page's font stack.
  3. Density on mobile fix78% of sessions are Australian mobile. Long 17px serif paragraphs on black are hard going. The founder story deserves its page; on the product page it should be one pull quote and a link.
  4. Single-product clutter fixRemove the variant picker, quantity selector and badges from the product page and the home featured product. One product, one price, one button is the "elevated essentials" promise made visible.
  5. The hero is a still upgradev3 uses a PNG; v4 uses a packshot. Either way it's static. The hero is where the 3D turntable or a six-second CGI loop belongs.

04 · The field

The top-right quadrant is empty.

Twenty-eight brands checked across Australia, the United States, the United Kingdom and Europe. The category splits four ways and none of the four is where TEL is standing.

Four camps, none of them yours

  • Pharmacy clinical. Bepanthen (A$13 for 50g in Chemist Warehouse), TattooMed, Delab. Blue-and-white, dermatologically tested, sold on price and ubiquity.
  • Supermarket natural. Dr Pickles (A$22.50 in Coles and Woolworths, rated 9.2 by GLOW), Ink Nurse (A$34.99 in 600-plus Chemist Warehouse stores), Aussie Inked. Australian-made, vegan, tubs and tubes.
  • Studio workhorse. Hustle Butter (A$32.50 for 150ml), INK-EEZE, Redemption, ProTat (A$10 for 50g). Bought by artists in bulk, branded loud.
  • Organic apothecary. Aftercare Collective and For Another Self, both Melbourne, both already using "ritual" language. Kraft tins, pastel flavours, magnetic gift boxes. Charming, not luxury.
The finding that matters. No brand selling into Australia occupies premium price and luxury codes at the same time. Stories & Ink is the only scaled brand near it globally, and it is beauty-retail-coded for Target and Superdrug shelves, not black glass. The quadrant TEL is aiming at is genuinely open.

That matters for TEL: a giftable, skincare-coded ritual set is aimed better at that buyer than a utilitarian tub, and it argues for gifting and second-purchase mechanics from day one rather than as an afterthought.

What to watch. Three pressures are real. Harry's launched Tattoo Frost in 2026 at A$0.13 per ml explicitly priced against Hustle Butter and Papatui. Also true: a private-label supplier in Australia will sell anyone a tattoo balm. Formula alone is not a moat. The moat is the studio, the founder's body, the object, and the refusal to discount.

Claims: what you can and cannot say in Australia

A tattoo aftercare cream is a cosmetic under Australian law (AICIS for ingredients, ACCC for advertising) as long as it is presented as cleansing, moisturising, protecting or improving the appearance of skin. It becomes a therapeutic good needing ARTG listing the moment it claims to prevent, treat or cure, or to influence a physiological process. Bepanthen is ARTG-registered, which is the only reason it can say "regenerate".

Your copy is already careful. Keep it that way. The founder's rework story stays a personal story, never a product claim.

Never on packaging, site or ads: heals, healing, speeds healing, treats, repairs, regenerates, antibacterial, antiseptic, kills germs, prevents infection, reduces scarring, prevents scabbing, anti-inflammatory, medical grade, clinically proven, any SPF or UV claim, wound, first aid.

Safe and still strong: soothes, hydrates, nourishes, helps maintain the skin's moisture barrier, protects, supports skin through the settling weeks, keeps fresh ink comfortable, helps ink look vivid, dermatologically tested (only with a test on file).

Labelling: full INCI list in descending order, country of origin, and the "Australian Made" logo only under licence. Beeswax rules out "vegan" for Restore Balm and petrolatum rules out "natural". Don't claim either. You don't need them.

06 · Imagery and 3D

Direct, clean, and insane: in that order.

Shopify supports 3D natively. Upload a .glb for the web and a .usdz for iPhone to the product's media and Prestige renders an interactive model in the gallery with a "View in your space" AR button on phones. No app, no custom code.

  1. Commission one photoreal modelBox plus both jars, built in Blender or Cinema 4D from the packaging dieline and label artwork. A$600–1,800, one to two weeks. This one asset produces everything below.
  2. Render the hero loopSix to eight second turntable, MP4 and WebM under 3 MB, for the home hero. Slow rotation, one gold rim light, black glass catching it. That is the "direct, clean" hero.
  3. Export GLB + USDZInto the product gallery as image five. Buyers on phones can spin the set and place it on their bathroom shelf.
  4. Render the scenesFour to six CGI stills: obsidian slab, wet black stone, gold ring light, mist. The Firefly concept in this repo is the right mood; the difference is that the labels will be pixel-accurate. That is the "insane" layer, and it doubles as your ad creative at 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16.
  5. Shoot what CGI cannot fakeHalf a day with a skincare texture photographer: balm being scooped, the cream's surface, both textures side by side, the founder's chest and back under studio light, ink at day 1 / day 14 / day 90.
  6. Phase two, post-launchA scroll-driven 3D product story in a custom section (Spline or Three.js). A$3–8k. Worth it once traffic is real, not before.
Why CGI over AI image generation for the product itself. Generated images garble label text and jar proportions. For a brand whose pitch is "built to a standard", the label on the hero must be the real label. Use AI for mood boards and background plates only.

11 · The gap

Elevated essentials, and the three refusals that protect it.

The niche is real and it is measurable: premium price with luxury codes, in Australia, in tattoo aftercare. Nobody is standing there. Holding it is a matter of discipline more than spend.

What you own that nobody can copy

  • A working studio. Hundreds of fresh pieces a year walk back through the door. That is a permanent product-testing and content pipeline no DTC brand can buy.
  • The founder's own body. A back removed by laser and reworked over three days with two artists; a chest rebuilt across five full-day sessions. That is the proof the entire category lacks, and it is not reproducible.
  • The object. Black glass, gold foil, a sealed box. In a market of tubs, tubes and kraft tins, the packaging alone does the positioning work.
  • The two-stage system. Seal, then condition. Everyone else pairs a cleanser with a moisturiser. Your structure maps to the healing timeline and gives the price a reason.
  • Artists in the room. Australian studio recommendations are fragmented and artist-by-artist. An artist-founded brand with a pro tier converts that into distribution without fighting Chemist Warehouse for shelf.

The three refusals

Every competitor in the category does all three of these. The moment TEL does any of them, the position collapses and it becomes another balm brand with nice packaging.

  1. No discount codes on the productMad Rabbit runs escalating subscription discounts and build-your-own bundles. Ink Nurse runs a Mega Bundle at A$139.99 against A$220 of "value". Discounting teaches the buyer to wait. The line is already on your page: no sales, no starter sizes, no half measures.
  2. No single jarsThe moment the balm is sold alone, the system argument dies and you are competing with Dr Pickles at A$22.50 in Coles. Sealed, never split, is the product.
  3. No merch bundlingCaps, singlets and stickers belong to the studio and to later chapters, not stapled to Chapter One to lift an order value.
Where the growth comes from instead. Not discounting: a Recovery Cream refill subscription (nobody in Australia runs one), gifting with a note or engraving, the studio pro tier, and the second, third and fourth chapters. Movement, Threads, Vision and Collective are already sitting there as empty collections waiting to be earned.

The brand is a nine. The site is a six wearing it. Six days is enough to close that.

# TEL Collection · Chapter One · Post-launch review (5 Oct 2026)

Review 1 of the TEL scoreboard. Window: Thu 10 Sep (doors open) to Mon 5 Oct, 25 days.
Sources: Shopify (orders, ShopifyQL sessions), Klaviyo campaign and flow reports (conversion metric: Placed Order), Meta Ads via Supermetrics (both ad accounts), Instagram Insights (TEL account). The live site could not be loaded from this sandbox, so the site review is from the published theme's templates (v7.1 "routine aligned (3 Oct)", theme 143754526783) and the launch-week renders.

## The one-line verdict

The counter sells. The website does not. Four weeks in, the studio counter has moved 36 sets and the online store has moved 7, off nearly 3,000 sessions. Everything below is about fixing that gap and turning the one thing that works (the counter) into the distribution model.

## 3. Website (v7.1 live theme)

What the live home page does, top to bottom: hero (scene photo) → founder → chair (the artists) → aim → **product** → spec (disabled) → steps (disabled) → proof (disabled) → reviews → chapter → Chapter Two newsletter.

What works:
- The brand tone is consistent across hero, founder, chair and chapter bands. Nobody would mistake this for a generic aftercare store.
- Announcement bar rotates three real messages (now open / 5.0 from the first five hundred / Chapter Two in development).
- PDP is complete: price, Judge.me badge, lead, spec, credential box, buy buttons, assurance, facts, accordions (ritual, routine, standard, stockists, founder, shipping), steps, proof, reviews, capture, chair, FAQ.
- Judge.me, Klaviyo onsite and Clarity are all enabled. Clarity recordings exist for the whole period and should be watched before any redesign.

What is costing orders:
1. **Product is the fifth band.** On a phone the Ritual Duo first appears about four screens down, after three story bands. Organic home-page visitors (1,019 sessions) have to want it badly. Move tel_product to position two, directly under the hero, or give the hero a direct "Shop the Duo" button to the PDP.
2. **Spec, steps and proof are disabled.** These were the three bands that answer "what is in it, how do I use it, why should I believe you". The v6.1 audit scored proof as the strongest conversion element on the page. Turning them off made the home page prettier and less persuasive.
3. **No sticky buy control.** On mobile, once past the product band there is no way back to purchase without scrolling up. A slim sticky bar (Ritual Duo · $59.95 · Shop) fixes this with zero design cost.
4. **Chapter Two newsletter band is competing with Chapter One.** The last thing on the page asks for a fragrance waitlist signup, not a purchase. Fine once Chapter One is sold through; premature at 410 sets.
5. **Stockists accordion on the PDP.** There is no stockist plan (section 7), so the accordion invites a conversation you do not want. Remove it.

Style score stays high. Conversion structure is what needs the work, and it is a one-evening change in the theme editor.

## 7. Stockists (the real lever)

One counter, no marketing, 36 sets in 25 days. The product sells where tattoos are done. Every other studio on the Gold Coast is that same counter.

Rough maths at the current rate: 1 studio ≈ 10 sets a week ≈ $600 retail. Ten stockists at a 40% wholesale margin ≈ $3,600 a week wholesale revenue to TEL, and the first 500 sets are gone in about six weeks instead of 37.

Nothing exists for this yet: no wholesale price, no stockist page, no one-pager, no counter display spec. The "stockists" accordion on the PDP is a promise with nothing behind it.

Ben's "$14 off the top of my head" is right. Note that packaging is more than half of it: the jars and formulas are A$6.65, the presentation is A$5.50. At 2,000 sets the landed cost only drops to about A$13.00, so the volume break is not worth the cash tied up.

Cash to place the 1,000 run: product A$6,650, freight ~A$1,200, metal cards A$2,860, cards and satchels ~A$2,700, GST at the border ~A$800 (claimed back). About **A$14,200**, of which A$3,300 is the deposit. Revenue at full sell-through: A$54,500 ex GST. Gross margin about 75%.

**Timing.** 410 sets on hand at ~11 sets a week runs out around June 2027. Ben has told VOG the order comes early 2027. Chinese New Year is 6 Feb 2027 and the factory has not committed to closure dates. To land before CNY, the deposit needs to be paid by early December with samples already approved. The alternative is a March order, landing by sea in May 2027, which is tight if online sales lift. Decision date Ben set himself: Fri 16 Oct.

**Open risk: the reformulation.** The 1 Oct email asks VOG for a lighter, fragrance-free Restore Balm and a Recovery Cream without vanilla, colourant, PEG emulsifiers or methylparaben. That is the right direction for a brand that publishes its lists, but it means run two is a different product from the one with twelve five-star reviews. It needs: samples tested on clients, a composition sheet per product, new INCI on the jar, fresh COAs, and the preservative-efficacy and accelerated-stability tests the factory does not hold (commission locally, budget ~A$1,500 to A$3,000 a product). Those tests take 8 to 12 weeks. Started now, they finish by mid-December, which is the same deadline as the pre-CNY deposit. Started later, run two is a May 2027 landing.
